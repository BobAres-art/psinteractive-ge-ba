Elena's Productive Workday

Read the profile below. Use the filter controls underneath to highlight how affirmative statements, negatives, and questions function naturally in daily narratives.

Elena is an architect living in Milan. She wakes up at 6:30 AM every morning and drinks black coffee with breakfast. She does not take the underground; instead, she cycles to her studio because she enjoys fresh air. At work, Elena designs sustainable housing projects and discusses blueprints with her team. She does not check personal emails during design sessions. At 1:00 PM, she has lunch with colleagues, but she does not eat at her desk.

Her manager often asks: "Do you finish the drafts before Friday?" Elena usually smiles and replies: "Yes, our team works quickly, and my assistant helps with 3D renderings." In the evening, she does not watch television. She reads architecture journals or relaxes in the garden. How does she manage everything? She plans her routine with care.

Present Simple: Core Rules & Endings

The present simple describes regular habits, permanent facts, and standard routines.

1. Affirmative & Negative Formations

Subject Affirmative (+) Negative (-)
I / You / We / They work / live / cycle do not (don't) work
He / She / It works / lives / cycles does not (doesn't) work

2. Third Person (-s / -es / -ies) Spelling Rules

Verb Ending Rule Examples
Most verbs Add -s read → reads, drink → drinks
-ch, -sh, -ss, -x, -o Add -es watch → watches, relax → relaxes, go → goes
Consonant + -y Change -y to -ies study → studies, carry → carries
Vowel + -y Add -s (regular) play → plays, enjoy → enjoys
Irregular: have Changes to has have → has

3. Questions & Short Answers

Question Pattern Short Answer (+) Short Answer (-)
Do you / they drink coffee? Yes, I / they do. No, I / they don't.
Does he / she wake up early? Yes, he / she does. No, he / she doesn't.
Where do you work? I work in an architecture studio.
