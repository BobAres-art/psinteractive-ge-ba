Chart Comparison Grammar

Comparing Numbers & Amounts in Pie Chart Reports

When describing a pie chart about food and drink spending, writers compare nouns using fewer, less, and more, and describe extreme categories with superlative adjectives using the most + adjective and the least + adjective (e.g., the most popular, the least common).

The pie chart illustrates the proportion of weekly household spending on five food and drink categories. Overall, meat and fish were the most popular and the most common category at 30%, whereas cakes and biscuits were the least popular choice at just 10%.

Shoppers purchased more fresh vegetables (25%) than milk and dairy products (20%). In contrast, households bought fewer soft drinks (15%) and even fewer cakes and biscuits (10%), making sweet snacks the least common purchase. Similarly, consumers spent less milk money (20%) than meat money (30%), showing that families consumed less dairy than meat overall.

fewer + plural noun

fewer soft drinks, fewer biscuits (we can count them: 1, 2, 3...)

less + uncountable noun

less dairy, less meat, less money (cannot count individually)

the most / the least + adj.

the most popular (30%) & the least common (10%) before adjectives

🔢 Countable Plural Nouns

  • • fewer + plural noun: People bought fewer biscuits than apples.
  • • more + plural noun: Families ate more vegetables than cakes.

🥛 Uncountable Nouns

  • • less + uncountable noun: Households drank less milk than water.
  • • more + uncountable noun: People spent more money on meat.

🏆 Superlative Adjectives

  • • the most + adjective: Meat was the most popular choice (30%).
  • • the least + adjective: Cakes were the least common item (10%).
