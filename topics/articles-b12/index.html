Articles Masterclass

Mastering a / an / the

Review the core rules of English articles to improve your grammar and accuracy, especially for formal writing like IELTS.

We use the indefinite article when referring to a singular noun which is not specified or mentioned for the first time. Uncountable nouns do not use a or an.

When to use "a"

  • Single objects or people: Owning a car
  • Non-specific objects: Go to a doctor
  • Professions: He is a teacher
  • Before a 'u' pronounced /ju:/: a university, a unique opportunity

When to use "an"

  • Before vowel sounds (/æ/, /e/, /ɪ/, /ə/, /ɒ/, /ʌ/): an apple
  • Before a silent 'h': an hour, an honest answer

Used for specific or defined persons/objects, even in plural.

  • ✓
    Specific Person/Object: The applicant with the most experience...
  • ✓
    Already Mentioned: ...hold a joint meeting. The meeting would be...
  • ✓
    Geography & Places: Mountain ranges (The Andes), oceans, rivers, island groups (The Maldives), museums. (Note: single mountains/lakes usually take no article).
  • ✓
    Groups of People: The wealthy, the young, the English.
  • ✓
    Unique Things: The moon, the Earth, the world.
  • ✓
    Definite Noun Phrases: the + Noun + of (The idea of...), the + Noun + to + Verb (The motivation to succeed...)

We often use no article when referring to things in a general way rather than being specific.

General Things (Plural / Uncountable)

Use plural noun or uncountable noun with NO article.

  • Cars are popular... (Countable plural)
  • Traffic is a problem... (Uncountable)
  • Happiness is important. (Abstract)

Proper Nouns & Categories

  • Names, cities, single lakes: London, Mount Fuji
  • Languages/Subjects: English, Science
  • Transport modes: by car, by train
  • Places by function: go to school, go to prison
Fixed use of THE
  • the key to success
  • the same as
  • behind the times
  • on the whole
  • in the long run
  • on the other hand
Fixed use of A / AN
  • to play a role
  • at a loss
  • to keep an eye on
  • a means to an end
  • a question of time
Fixed use of NO ARTICLE
  • to be at war / peace
  • word of mouth
  • all day
  • in other words
  • to make sense

The word government is very common in IELTS essays. Because it can be countable, it takes different articles depending on the context.

1. The government (Definite Article)

Use the when referring to a specific social institution or the specific ruling body of a particular country (or the one you are living in/writing about).

Example: "The government should invest more money in public healthcare."

2. A government (Indefinite Article)

Use a when referring to any single, unspecified government, or a specific type of government.

Example: "A government that ignores the needs of its people will eventually lose support."

3. Governments (Plural / No Article)

Use the plural form with no article when talking about governments in general across the world.

Example: "Governments around the world must work together to tackle climate change."

4. Government (Uncountable / No Article)

Use no article when referring to the abstract concept, system, or action of governing, rather than a specific group of people.

Example: "The role of government in society is constantly debated."
