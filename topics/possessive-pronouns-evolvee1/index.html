Part 3 • Grammar Discovery

Rules of Possession & Question Words

Observe how English signals ownership. Compare forms side-by-side, discover the golden differences, and test out how sentences transform.

RULE 1

Possessive Adjectives vs. Possessive Pronouns

Adjectives need a noun after them. Pronouns replace the noun completely!

Subject Possessive Adjective Followed by a noun Possessive Pronoun Stands on its own Example in Workplace
I my laptop mine "This is my desk." → "This desk is mine."
you your tablet yours "Is this your pen?" → "Is this pen yours?"
he his ruler his "It is his blueprint." → "The blueprint is his."
she her camera hers "That's her office." → "That office is hers."
we our studio ours "This is our project." → "This project is ours."
they their keys theirs "These are their tools." → "These tools are theirs."
! Memory trick: Most possessive pronouns end with -s (yours, hers, ours, theirs), except mine and his!
Contraction Who's

Who's = Who is

Contains an apostrophe representing the missing letter i. It always asks about the identity of a person doing something or located somewhere.

• "Who's coming to the meeting?" (= Who is coming)

• "Who's from Puebla? Cecilia." (= Who is from Puebla)

Test: Can you say "Who is"? If YES → use Who's.
Possession Whose

Whose = Belonging to which person

Has NO apostrophe. It asks who owns or has a relationship with something or someone.

• "Whose bag is this?" (= Which person owns this bag?)

• "Whose family lives in Puebla? Cecilia's."

Test: Can you say "Who is"? If NO → use Whose.
RULE 3

The Apostrophe -'s & Alternative Phrases

We add 's to a person's name to show possession. In conversational English, there are two equivalent ways to ask about and state ownership:

Asking about ownership:

• "Whose is this?" / "Whose bag is this?"

• "Who does this bag belong to?"

Both questions have the exact same meaning!
Answering with -'s or pronoun:

• "It's Elena's." / "It's my son's keychain."

• "It's mine." / "It belongs to me."

Note: my son's keychain = the keychain that belongs to my son.
