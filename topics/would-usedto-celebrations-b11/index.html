Memories of Celebrations

Exploring past habits, family traditions, and festive seasons with used to and would

Festive Days from My Childhood

Read the personal essay below about traditional New Year and Midwinter village festivals. Notice how the writer describes memories that repeated year after year.

When I was ten years old, winter was the most magical season of the year. Our whole village used to look forward to the annual Midwinter Festival for months. My grandfather used to own a small bakery near the central square, and during the holidays, everyone in the neighborhood gathered there.

Every morning in December, we would wake up before sunrise to help him bake honey bread. The kitchen used to smell of cinnamon, cloves, and burnt orange peel. My cousins and I would roll the dough into stars, and grandfather would sing old folk melodies while tending the wood oven.

People in our village used to believe that sharing bread brought good luck for the harvest. In the evenings, musicians would gather in the square, and families would dance around the bonfire until midnight. Life used to feel much simpler back then, but those festive traditions stayed in our hearts forever.

Action Verbs vs. State (Stative) Verbs

To choose accurately between used to and would, we must identify the type of verb we are using:

Action Verbs (Dynamic)

Describe physical actions and activities that have a beginning and an end.

dance cook visit sing bake travel
State Verbs (Stative)

Describe situations, feelings, ownership, beliefs, and conditions that continue over time.

be have (own) love believe seem understand

Comparing "Used to" vs. "Would"

Feature used to + base verb would + base verb
Repeated Past Actions ✔ Yes
"We used to visit our cousins every festival."		 ✔ Yes
"We would visit our cousins every festival."
Past States / Situations ✔ Yes
"I used to love street carnivals."
"There used to be a fair here."		 ❌ NEVER with states
"I would love street carnivals." (Incorrect)
Questions & Negatives Common & natural:
"We didn't use to buy costumes."
"Did you use to celebrate?"		 Rare/uncommon for past habit.
Usually restricted to affirmative sentences in storytelling.
Time Anchor Requirement Can establish the past time independently without prior mention. Usually requires a clear past time frame already established in the story.
Key Rule of Thumb: When in doubt with past habits, used to is almost always grammatically safe. Would is only possible when describing repeated actions, never states!

Discussing Past Celebrations

Use these prompts to talk about celebrations in your culture, town, or family. Focus on using used to for past realities or beliefs, and would when narrating repeated festive actions.

🗣️ Childhood Festivities: What is one festival you celebrated every year as a child? What food would your family prepare? Who used to attend?

🗣️ Lost Customs: Think of a tradition that has disappeared. (e.g., "People used to send physical cards, but now everyone sends a text message.")

🗣️ Action Narration: Describe a typical festival morning when you were younger. Use would at least three times to describe the routine: (e.g., "We would wake up early, my father would brew cardamom tea, and we would visit our grandparents.")
