A Tale of Two Days

Every day, I wake up at 7:00 AM. I do not eat a big breakfast. I just drink coffee. Does my brother drink coffee? No, he doesn't. He drinks tea. We usually clean the house together in the afternoon.

But yesterday, things were different! I didn't wake up at 7:00 AM. I woke up at 9:00 AM because I was tired. I ate a huge breakfast with eggs and toast. Did my brother drink tea yesterday? Yes, he did, but he also drank orange juice. We didn't clean the house. We watched a movie instead.

The Rules of Time

Click on the sections below to reveal how we build sentences in both tenses.

Present Simple

For habits, routines, and things that are always true.

Subject + Verb (s/es for He/She/It)

  • I play tennis.
  • She plays tennis.

Subject + do/does not + Base Verb

  • I do not (don't) eat meat.
  • He does not (doesn't) eat meat.

Do/Does + Subject + Base Verb?

  • Do you like coffee?
  • Does she like coffee?

Past Simple

For finished actions that happened in the past.

Subject + Past Verb (V2)

  • I watched TV yesterday. (Regular)
  • She ate pizza last night. (Irregular)

Subject + did not + Base Verb

Notice: We use the base verb here, NOT the past verb!

  • We did not (didn't) go out.
  • He didn't play football.

Did + Subject + Base Verb?

Again, use the base verb after 'Did'!

  • Did you see that?
  • Did they arrive on time?

Making the Past

In the past tense, verbs belong to two families. Regular verbs follow the rules and just add -ed at the end. Irregular verbs break the rules and change completely!
