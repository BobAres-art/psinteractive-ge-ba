How to Write a Report for Pie Charts

Learn how to describe pie charts clearly, logically, and accurately. Master the 4-paragraph method, key comparison phrases, and simple sentence structures.

20 Minutes

Formula for Success

The 4-Paragraph Structure

Organize your report logically every single time using this reliable sequence:

1

Introduction

Paraphrase the prompt in 1 sentence. Tell the reader what the charts illustrate, the location, and the time period.

💡 "The pie charts illustrate the proportion of..."
2

Overview

Provide 2 to 3 main general points: Which category is the biggest? Smallest? Which grew or dropped the most? (Do not list numbers here!)

💡 "Overall, heating accounted for the largest share..."
3

Detail Paragraph 1

Present specific data, figures, and percentages for the major categories or primary sectors. Make clean comparisons.

💡 "Looking at heating first, it comprised 42%..."
4

Detail Paragraph 2

Present figures for the remaining categories (e.g., smaller sectors, stable categories). Compare numbers accurately.

💡 "In contrast, lighting was the smallest sector at only..."

Step 2: Complete Model Report

Sample Task Model Answer

Notice how each paragraph connects back to the features you identified above.

Paragraph 1: Introduction 1 Sentence

The two pie charts compare the proportion of energy used for different purposes in an average European household in 2010 and 2020.

Why this works: "The two pie charts compare the proportion of..." rephrases the prompt cleanly. It states what, where, and when accurately.
Paragraph 2: Overview 2 Sentences

Overall, heating rooms remained the largest category of energy use in both years, whereas lighting consistently accounted for the smallest share. In addition, while energy used for water heating and cooling increased, the proportions for room heating and appliances declined.

Why this works: Highlights the dominant category (room heating), the minor category (lighting), and broad changes (water heating and cooling up, others down) without listing any figures.
Paragraph 3: Major Sectors Room & Water Heating

In 2010, space heating was by far the largest energy expense, making up 52% of the total. By 2020, this figure dropped slightly to 45%, but it still remained the highest. In contrast, the percentage of energy spent on water heating rose moderately from 20% in 2010 to 25% in 2020, solidifying its position as the second largest consumer of energy.

Why this works: Compares the two leading categories with exact percentages ("52%", "45%", "20%", "25%") and clear transitional words ("In contrast").
Paragraph 4: Remaining Sectors Appliances, Lighting & Cooling

Regarding other household needs, household appliances represented 16% of energy use in 2010, which decreased to 12% ten years later. Meanwhile, cooling started at only 4% in 2010 and doubled to 8% in 2020. Finally, the percentage for lighting showed very little change, remaining nearly unchanged at roughly 8% to 10% in both decades.

Why this works: Clearly describes the three smaller categories using cohesive links ("Regarding...", "Meanwhile...", "Finally...").

Crucial Grammar Rule

"Percent" vs. "Percentage"

Many learners confuse these two words. Here is the simple golden rule to keep your writing accurate.

Percent (%)

Always with a Number

Use percent immediately following a specific number or quantity. Do not add an "s" to percent.

✅ "Heating accounted for 45 percent of all energy."
✅ "Only 8 percent was used for cooling."
❌ "A high percent of people..." (Incorrect!)
Formula: [Number] + percent

Percentage

General Noun

Use percentage as a normal noun when there is NO specific number right before it. Often used with the, a large, a small.

✅ "The percentage of energy for cooling increased."
✅ "A large percentage of the budget went to heating."
❌ "It stood at 45 percentage." (Incorrect!)
Formula: [The / A large / A small] + percentage of...

Sentence Starters Cheat Sheet

Keep these natural, error-free phrases in mind when writing your own reports:

For Introductions

  • • The pie chart provides information about...
  • • The charts compare the proportion of...
  • • The diagrams illustrate how... was distributed in...

For Overviews

  • • Overall, it is clear that... represented the largest share.
  • • In general,... accounted for the smallest proportion.
  • • While... experienced an upward trend,... declined.

For Describing Data

  • • ...made up roughly [X] percent of the total.
  • • ...constituted the second largest category at [X]%.
  • • In contrast,... rose moderately from [X]% to [Y]%.
