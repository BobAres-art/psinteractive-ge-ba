Every year, thousands of university students think about their future career. How will work change over the next ten years? Many career counselors agree that automation and green energy will change the world economy. Some traditional office tasks will not exist in their current form, but new professions will replace them.

Maria is studying environmental science. When people ask her about her plans, she explains: "Maybe I'll become a sustainable architect, or I might join a global clean energy company. I will probably move to another city for my first internship. My parents wonder if I will be OK with living alone, and I always answer: Yes, I will! It will possibly feel stressful at first, but it will definitely teach me independence."

Her friend Liam is learning computer programming. He has slightly different predictions: "I won't work in a noisy open office every day. Remote positions are growing quickly. I may specialize in cyber defense, or I might start my own app startup with two classmates. We will probably finish our main certification this winter. What will you do after you graduate? Nobody can know every detail, but good preparation always creates exciting options."

Part 2 • Guided Discovery

Notice the Speaker's Certainty

When we speak about the future, we often express different degrees of certainty. Read the statements below and discover how word choices show how sure someone feels.

High Certainty 100% Sure

"New professions will replace them."

The speaker feels confident about this future fact or prediction.

Pattern: will + base verb
Possibility ~50% Sure

"I might join a clean energy company."

The speaker is thinking of an open option; it is not guaranteed.

Pattern: might / may + base verb
Likely Chance 75% - 80% Sure

"We will probably finish our certification."

The speaker thinks it is very likely, but leaves a small room for doubt.

Pattern: will + probably + base verb

Base Form After Modals

Always use the base verb (infinitive without 'to') after will, won't, might, and may. Never add -s, -ed, or -ing.

✓ She will start tomorrow.
✗ She will starts tomorrow.

Word Order with Adverbs

When you aren't completely sure, place probably or possibly before the main verb:

  • • You'll probably feel stressed.
  • • You'll possibly feel stressed.
  • • Use maybe at the beginning of the sentence:
    "Maybe I'll go out with friends after work."
