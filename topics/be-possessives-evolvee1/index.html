This is my older sister, Sara. She is twenty-two years old, and she is a student at the university. Her room is upstairs. She is very neat, and her books are always tidy on her shelf. Next is my brother, Leo. He is only ten years old. His favorite toy is a red bicycle. He is often in the garden with our dog, Buster. Buster is small, and its tail never stops wagging!

Part 2 • Grammar Rules

Grammar Reference: Verb 'BE' & Possessive Adjectives

Review the basic forms below. Look at the examples about family members, friends, and everyday items.

Subject Affirmative (+) Negative (-) Question (?) Short Answers
I am from Indiana.
(I'm)		 'm not from Florida. Am I late? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
He / She / It is my roommate.
(He's / She's / It's)		 's not my boyfriend. / isn't my boyfriend. Is he / she / it from Indiana? Yes, she is. / No, she isn't (or she's not).
You / We / They are close friends.
(You're / We're / They're)		 're not close friends. / aren't close friends. Are they your neighbors? Yes, they are. / No, they're not (or they aren't).

💡 Notice: Two Natural Ways to Say "Not"

People use both styles every day. Both are correct:

Style 1: • you're not
• we're not
• they're not
• he's / she's / it's not
Style 2: • you aren't
• we aren't
• they aren't
• he / she / it isn't

🔑 Subject Pronoun → Possessive Adjective

Possessive adjectives come before a noun to say whose family member or thing it is:

I → my sister
You → your brother
He → his father
She → her mother
It → its toy
We → our house
They → their parents
