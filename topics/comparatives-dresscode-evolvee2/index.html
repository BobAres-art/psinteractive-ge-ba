Office Dress Code Discussion

Read the conversation between Clara and Leo discussing the company's new hybrid wardrobe policy.

Clara: Hi Leo! Are you wearing running shoes with a blazer? That looks more modern than standard business suits, but is it allowed?
Leo: Yes! The management introduced a new dress guide yesterday. Everyday office wear is now more casual than before.
Clara: That is great news. High heels are nicer than sneakers for events, but flat shoes are definitely more comfortable than heels at my standing desk.
Leo: Exactly. And my dark jeans look better than my faded ones, so they still look smart. Plus, getting dressed in the morning is now easier than ever.
Clara: True! A simple leather belt makes this outfit classier than a plain sport look. However, meeting clients is still more formal than regular work days.
Leo: Of course. We don't want our client presentations to look worse than our competitors' presentations!

1. One-Syllable Adjectives

Add -er + than to short adjectives.

Adjective Type Rule Example
General 1-syllable + -er smart → smarter than
Ending in -e + -r nice → nicer than
Consonant + Vowel + Consonant (CVC) Double final letter + -er big → bigger than

2. Two-Syllable Adjectives Ending in -y

Change -y to -ier + than.

Base Adjective Comparative Form Example Sentence
easy easier Linen is easier to wash than silk.
classy classier A watch looks classier than a plastic bracelet.

3. Long Adjectives (2+ Syllables, not ending in -y)

Use more + adjective + than. Do not modify the ending of the adjective.

Adjective Comparative Form Example
formal more formal Suits are more formal than hoodies.
comfortable more comfortable Sneakers are more comfortable than boots.
expensive more expensive Gold watches are more expensive than silver ones.

4. Irregular Comparatives

These adjectives completely change their form. Memorize them:

Adjective Comparative Context
good better than This jacket is better for cold offices.
bad worse than Wrinkled shirts look worse than casual t-shirts.
far farther / further than The outlet store is further than the mall.
Important Word Order Reminder: Always remember to place than directly before the second object or person you are comparing!
