1. Prepositions of Value, Target, and Difference

Preposition Rule & Structure Correct Example Common Error to Avoid
at Specific level, peak, or stable point.
peaked / stood / remained + at 		"Sales peaked at 4,000 units."
"The rate stood at 20% in May." 		peaked to 4,000
stood in 20%
to Final destination / end number.
rose / fell / dropped + to 		"Sales fell to 1,500 units."
"The figure increased to 80%." 		fell at 1,500 (when 1,500 is the final number)
by Size of difference after a verb.
[Verb] + by + [amount] 		"Sales fell by 25%."
"Profits grew by $2 million." 		fell of 25%
dropped down 25%
of Size of difference after a noun.
[Noun] + of + [amount] 		"There was a rise of 60%."
"The company saw a decline of 10%." 		an increase by 60%
a decline in 10%
from ... to Starting point to finishing point.
from [X] to [Y] 		"Sales rose from 2,500 to 4,000." rose from 2,500 until 4,000
from 2,500 and 4,000

2. Prepositions of Time, Duration, and Subject Matter

Preposition Rule & Function Correct Example Notes
in 1. Month, year, or season.
2. Area or topic of change. 		"A small rise in April."
"An increase in phone sales." 		Compare: increase of 60% (number) vs increase in sales (topic).
between ... and Two fixed time points.
between [A] and [B] 		"The lowest sales were between March and June." Never write: between March to June.
over A period of time during which a trend happens. "Over a six-month period, sales increased."
"Numbers rose over the next five years." 		Focuses on the change occurring across that span.
throughout Continuous from the start to the end of a period. "Prices fluctuated throughout the year."
"The rate stayed stable throughout 2010." 		Shows something happened all through the entire duration.

Contrast: TO vs BY

  • to = Resulting level: "Sales fell from 2,000 to 1,500." (1,500 is the final number).
  • by = Difference: "Sales fell by 500." (2,000 minus 1,500 = 500 difference).

Contrast: OVER vs THROUGHOUT

  • over = Across a timeframe: "Sales rose over a six-month period."
  • throughout = Non-stop or in all parts: "Figures remained flat throughout the year."
