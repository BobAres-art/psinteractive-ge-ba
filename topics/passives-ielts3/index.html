Phase 2: Master Blueprint

The Four Key Passive Formulas

The core formula of ANY passive is always: Be (in target tense) + Past Participle (V3).

Routine / Facts

Present Simple Passive

am / is / are + Past Participle

Used for regular routines, universal facts, and fixed protocols.

"The gallery doors are unlocked at 9:00 AM."
Active: Conservators inspect manuscripts → Passive: Manuscripts are inspected.
Completed Past

Past Simple Passive

was / were + Past Participle

Used for completed single events or actions at a specific past time.

"The lost canvas was discovered in 1994."
Active: Workers built the wing → Passive: The wing was built.
Happening Now

Present Continuous Passive

is / are + being + Past Participle

Used for actions actively taking place right around this exact moment.

"The portrait is being cleaned this week."
Active: They are restoring it → Passive: It is being restored.
Recent Result

Present Perfect Passive

has / have + been + Past Participle

Used for actions with direct consequence or news connected to now.

"All stolen pieces have been recovered."
Active: They have published results → Passive: Results have been published.

1. Receiver is the Focus

When the object is more important than who did it. e.g., "The cathedral was completed in 1345."

2. Agent is Obvious or Unknown

If everyone knows who does it, or nobody knows. e.g., "My car was stolen last night."

3. Objective & Formal Tone

Common in academic writing, journalism, and scientific papers. e.g., "The sample is heated to 100°C."
