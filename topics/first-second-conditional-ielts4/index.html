Venice & Rio Carnivals

Masks in Venice and Music in Rio

Elena is preparing for her winter trip to the Venice Carnival. She has packed warm coats, boots, and a decorative mask. She says: "If the sea water rises in the morning, I will take the wooden walking bridges across Saint Mark's Square." She knows the winter high tide is normal in Venice and the city always builds temporary raised bridges.

Later, while drinking hot chocolate, Elena laughs about the famous Rio Carnival in Brazil: "If I had wings, I would fly across the Atlantic ocean to dance on a parade float tonight." Of course, humans cannot grow wings, and she must go to work on Monday.

Story Content Question

What does Elena plan to do if the sea water rises in Saint Mark's Square?

Glowing Lanterns and Ice Castles

At the Yi Peng Festival in Chiang Mai, Thailand, Kenji holds a paper lantern with his family. The tour leader tells the group: "If you hold the lantern straight until the air gets warm, it will rise safely into the sky." Kenji holds it steady, and a moment later it lifts into the starry evening.

Afterwards, Kenji looks at travel photos of the Harbin Ice Festival in northeast China, where builders make giant castles out of thick river ice. He smiles: "If people lived in houses made of solid ice, we would melt our walls every time we made soup!" He knows people only visit the ice buildings for entertainment during the freezing winter.

Story Content Question

According to the tour leader in Chiang Mai, how do you make the paper lantern fly?

Buñol & Oaxaca

Red Streets in Spain and Orange Flowers in Mexico

In the small Spanish town of Buñol, crowds gather for the tomato fight, La Tomatina. The safety rules are simple and strict. An organizer announces: "If you squeeze the tomato before throwing it, you will not hurt anyone." Participants also wear swimming goggles to keep the sour tomato juice out of their eyes.

In Oaxaca, Mexico, families celebrate the Day of the Dead by decorating altars with bright marigold petals and favorite foods. Sofia sits with her grandmother and dreams: "If sweet festival bread rained from the sky, we would never need to bake again!" Her grandmother laughs and passes her the rolling pin so they can start baking bread together in the kitchen.

Story Content Question

Why do participants squeeze the tomatoes before throwing them in Buñol?

2

Form & Meaning Studio

Compare real future situations with improbable possibilities and completely unreal fantasies.

Sentence Transformer

Realistic Future Plan

First Conditional Realistic Opportunity (Possible Future)
Meaning Category: Real Life Action
Condition Clause (If...)
If I buy a ticket tomorrow,
Verb Form: Present Simple
Result Clause (Consequence)
I will visit the lantern festival.
Modal Form: will + base verb

First Conditional: Real, practical future situations. After if, use the Present Simple (buy). In the result clause, use will + base verb (will visit).

Second Conditional Nuance A: Improbable Situation

Technically possible in real life, but the probability is tiny: "If I won the grand lottery tomorrow, I would buy tickets for everyone."

Second Conditional Nuance B: Completely Unreal / Fantasy

Contrary to nature or human facts; 100% imaginary: "If humans had wings, we would fly above the lantern festival."

Likelihood Meter Likely & Real (~85%)

Use First Conditional when the condition is genuinely planned and likely to happen.

Clause Order & Comma Rule

If I buy a ticket, I will visit the lantern festival.

When the 'If' clause comes first, use a comma (,). When the result clause comes first, do not use a comma!
