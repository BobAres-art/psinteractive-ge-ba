Saturday Night Street Food Market

Liam: "Where should we get dinner? I prefer to eat at food trucks because I love to try new food."
S
Sara: "Food trucks sound great, but that popular taco truck is packed! I usually can't stand waiting in long lines."

Liam: "The spicy noodle stand has seats right now. I don't mind waiting here at this table if you want to order."
S
Sara: "Perfect. Later, do you want to watch a movie at my place?"
L
Liam: "I'd love that! I decided to leave my car at home, so we can walk over together."

1 Deduction: Expressing Intention & Decisions

Notice "decided" and "want" in the chat. What form followed them?

2 Deduction: Expressing Patience & Tolerating

Look at "can't stand" and "don't mind". What shape did the next verb take?

3 Deduction: Flexible Emotional Verbs

Liam said: "I love to try new food." Could he also say "I love trying new food" with the same meaning?

Discovery Rule: The first verb decides what follows it: some verbs demand to + verb, others require verb + -ing, and some allow both!

Core Foundations

Study the three primary verb behaviors in everyday English

Verb + to + Verb Infinitive

Used when expressing future goals, plans, choices, and decisions.

Target Examples

"I prefer to eat at food trucks."

"We decided to leave early."

Common Verbs:
want decide plan need hope
Verb + -ing Gerund

Used when talking about habits, ongoing experiences, tolerance, and attitudes.

Target Examples

"I can't stand waiting in line."

"I don't mind waiting here."

Common Verbs:
can't stand don't mind enjoy avoid finish
Both Patterns Dual Form

These verbs can take either pattern with virtually no difference in meaning.

Interchangeable

"I love to try new food."

= "I love trying new food."

Common Dual Verbs:
love like hate prefer start
Quick Reference Matrix

Everyday Sentence Structures

Category Target Verbs Affirmative Example (+) Negative / Question (?/-)
Verb + to + Verb want, decide, hope, need, plan I decided to leave early. Do you want to watch a film?
Verb + -ing can't stand, don't mind, enjoy, avoid I usually enjoy cooking at home. I can't stand waiting in line.
Both (Either) love, like, hate, prefer, start I prefer to eat at food trucks. I also prefer eating tacos.
Warning Alert Never use two bare base verbs: say "I decided to leave", never "I decided leave".
