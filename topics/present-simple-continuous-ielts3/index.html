Present Simple vs. Present Continuous

Understand when to use routine and permanent truths versus immediate moments, unfinished activities, and temporary life states.

Permanent & Habitual

Present Simple

Subject + Base Verb (+s/es)

  • Routines & habits: What you do consistently.
  • Facts & General Truths: Water boils at 100°C.
  • Opinions & Stative verbs: What you think or believe.
always usually every day never
Temporary & In Progress

Present Continuous

Subject + am/is/are + Verb-ing

  • Right now: Actions happening at the moment of speaking.
  • Temporary situations: Short-term conditions.
  • Incomplete/Ongoing: Reading a book these days.
now at the moment currently these days

Side-by-Side Visual Comparisons

See the real world difference through 4 decisive context pairings.

Context Pair 01

Repeated Actions vs. Happening at the Time of Speaking

Routine vs. This Exact Second
Present Simple
Habit & Frequency

"David runs every morning."

This is a regular scheduled routine. It does not mean he is running at this very moment; he could be eating cereal right now, but running is his daily habit.

Keywords: every day, usually, often
Present Continuous
Action in Progress

"Look! David is running across the bridge now."

David is actively on the pavement, feet in motion, at the precise moment you are looking at him and uttering the sentence.

Keywords: now, right now, listen!, look!
Context Pair 02

General Facts & Laws of Nature vs. Temporary Situations

Permanent Truth vs. Limited Duration
Present Simple
Universal Truth

"Snow melts when the temperature rises above 0°C."

This is a scientific reality that has always been and always will be true. It does not depend on today's weather forecast.

Concept: Timeless facts, geographical states
Present Continuous
Temporary Life Situation

"I am staying with my cousin this week while my flat is painted."

I do not live with my cousin permanently. It is a temporary situation that will end in a few days when the paint dries.

Keywords: this week, for now, temporarily
Context Pair 03

Actions in Progress Around Now (Incomplete) vs. Permanent Profession

Ongoing Project vs. Fixed State
Present Simple
Permanent Job / Identity

"Sarah teaches high school literature."

This is her career and permanent occupational state. Even during summer holidays or at night while asleep, she is still a teacher.

Concept: Long-term occupation, home country
Present Continuous
Incomplete Project Around Now

"Sarah is writing a novel this year."

The action is started but not yet completed. She might be having coffee right now, but in this general time period, she is actively composing a book.

Keywords: these days, this semester, currently
Context Pair 04

Opinions & Stative Verbs vs. Active Mental/Physical Actions

Internal State vs. Active Deliberation
Present Simple
Opinion / Belief (Stative)

"I think it is a great opportunity."

Here, "think" expresses an opinion or fixed view. Stative verbs describing beliefs, emotions, and senses are generally not used in the continuous form.

Never say: "I am thinking it is good."
Present Continuous
Active Deliberation / Process

"Be quiet, I am thinking about this math problem."

When "think" describes the mental process of contemplating, problem-solving, or pondering something actively, it becomes dynamic and continuous.

Valid because it is an active mental effort!

Grammar Rules & Form Formulas

Reference master table for Affirmative (+), Negative (−), and Question (?) patterns.

Present Simple Formulas

Used for repeated routines, facts, and permanent truths.

Affirmative (+) S + V1(+s/es)

I / You / We / They work.

He / She / It works.

Example: "She drinks green tea every morning."

Negative (−) S + do/does not + V(base)

I / You / We / They do not (don't) work.

He / She / It does not (doesn't) work.

Example: "He doesn't like spicy food." (Notice: base verb 'like', no 's')

Question (?) Do/Does + S + V(base)?

Do you / they play football?

Does he / she speak Spanish?

Short answers: "Yes, I do." / "No, he doesn't."

3rd Person (-s / -es) Spelling Rules:

• Most verbs add -s: work → works, live → lives

• Verbs ending in -ch, -sh, -s, -x, -z, -o add -es: watch → watches, go → goes

• Consonant + -y turns to -ies: study → studies (vowel + y: play → plays)

Present Continuous Formulas

Used for current actions, temporary situations, and ongoing plans.

Affirmative (+) S + am/is/are + V-ing

I am working ('m working).

He / She / It is working ('s working).

We / You / They are working ('re working).

Example: "We are studying for the test right now."

Negative (−) S + am/is/are + not + V-ing

I am not working.

He / She / It is not (isn't) working.

We / You / They are not (aren't) working.

Example: "They aren't listening to music."

Question (?) Am/Is/Are + S + V-ing?

Are you reading that newspaper?

Is it raining outside?

Short answers: "Yes, I am." / "No, she isn't."

-ing Spelling Rules:

• Silent -e drops: make → making, write → writing (except ee: see → seeing)

• 1 vowel + 1 consonant (C-V-C): Double final consonant: run → running, swim → swimming

• Verbs ending in -ie change to -y: die → dying, lie → lying

Stative Verb Warning: Verbs Not Normally Used in Continuous Tenses

Stative verbs describe a state (feelings, thoughts, possession, senses) rather than a physical action. Even when happening right now, use the Present Simple:

Emotion & Likes love, like, hate, prefer, adore, need, want
Thoughts & Beliefs know, believe, understand, remember, agree
Senses & Perception see, hear, smell, taste, seem, appear
Possession have (own), own, belong, possess, contain
