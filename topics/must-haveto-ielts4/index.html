Mastering Obligations: Must, Have to, Mustn't, and Don't have to

Orientation Day: New Internship Briefing

Read the conversation between Clara (a new intern) and Mr. Davis (the internship supervisor). Pay attention to how rules, responsibilities, and permissions are communicated.

Mr. Davis: Welcome to the team, Clara! Let’s cover a few essential office guidelines before you begin.

Clara: Thank you, Mr. Davis. What time do I need to be here in the morning?

Mr. Davis: Official work hours start at 9:00 AM. You have to arrive on time because our morning briefing starts promptly at 9:05 AM.

Clara: Understood. Do we follow a formal dress code every day?

Mr. Davis: On Mondays and client meeting days, you must wear business attire. However, on regular days, you don't have to wear a suit; smart casual is completely fine.

Clara: That sounds very reasonable. What about digital security?

Mr. Davis: This is crucial: you mustn't share client files on unsecured cloud drives or personal USBs. It is strictly against corporate data protection policies.

Clara: Got it. What if I finish my training modules early today?

Mr. Davis: If you complete them, you don't have to stay until 5:00 PM today. But remember, you must sign out at the front reception desk whenever you leave.

Grammar Rules: Positive vs. Negative

While both must and have to convey necessity in the affirmative, their negative forms carry entirely different meanings.

1. Positive: Obligation & Necessity

Structure Meaning Example
must + base verb Strong personal obligation or written official rule. You must sign out at the desk.
have to / has to + base verb External obligation (laws, timetables, work rules). We have to submit the report by 9 AM.

2. Negative: Prohibition vs. Lack of Obligation

Structure Meaning Example
mustn't + base verb
(must not)		 Prohibition (0% permission / forbidden / illegal). You mustn't share password files.
(Do not do it!)
don't / doesn't have to + base verb No obligation (optional / not necessary). You don't have to wear a suit today.
(You can if you want, but it is not required.)
Key Rule to Remember:
• Mustn't = Forbidden (Against the rules).
• Don't have to = Optional (Free choice, zero obligation).

Interactive Practice

2. Context Application

3. Fill in the Blank

4. Third-Person Agreement

Sarah is already fluent in French, so she take the introductory language module.

5. Safety Protocol

You _______ touch that electrical wire with wet hands. It is extremely hazardous!

Decision Lab: Real-World Scenarios

Read each workplace scenario and determine whether the action is strictly prohibited (mustn't) or optional (doesn't/don't have to).

Scenario A: Company Cafeteria

The office provides complimentary lunches for employees every weekday. Bringing your own lunch from home is permitted.

Conclusion: You buy food at the office cafeteria.

Scenario B: Laboratory Safety

Chemical vapors in the testing lab can cause serious respiratory harm without protective equipment.

Conclusion: Technicians enter the testing room without safety goggles and a respirator mask.

Scenario C: Remote Work Day

On Fridays, staff can work from home or use the desk spaces in the main building as they prefer.

Conclusion: Employees commute to the headquarters on Friday mornings.
