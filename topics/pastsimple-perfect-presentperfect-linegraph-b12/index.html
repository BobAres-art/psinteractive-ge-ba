Visual Data Analysis: Public Electric Vehicle (EV) Adoption

Examine the multi-decade trajectory below (1990–Present Day). Pay close attention to how the report switches tense depending on chronological milestones and current relevance.

The line graph illustrates the total adoption numbers of public electric fleet vehicles across two distinct metropolises from 1990 up to the present day. Overall, both cities experienced sustained growth throughout the duration, though Metro A outpaced Metro B markedly after introducing municipal subsidies.


In 1990, registrations in both territories stood at negligible levels, remaining under 5,000 units each. Prior to the policy revision of 2015, uptake in Metro A had remained largely static for over a decade. However, once regional tax rebates took effect that same year, deployment in Metro A surged past Metro B, climbing swiftly from 40,000 to approximately 75,000 vehicles by 2020. Before this acceleration occurred, planners had prioritized traditional infrastructure over clean fleet charging corridors.


Since 2020, investment in Metro A has accelerated considerably, and total active registrations have reached a record summit of nearly 100,000 units. Concurrently, Metro B has seen a steady yet more moderate climb, crossing 70,000 recently.

Past Simple: Fixed past intervals & milestones (e.g., in 1990, in 2015, by 2020).
Past Perfect Simple: Anterior state completed before another past pivot point (e.g., prior to 2015... had remained).
Present Perfect: Trend originating in the past and continuing up to the moment of writing (e.g., since 2020... has reached).

The "By + Past Year" Fallacy:
Examiners regularly penalize candidates who confuse In 2010 with By 2010.
• In 2010, output reached 50 tonnes. (Point in time → Past Simple)
• By 2010, output had reached 50 tonnes. (Cumulative ceiling achieved before 2010 → Past Perfect)
