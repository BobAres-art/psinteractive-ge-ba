Real-World Context

A Day in the Life: Different Jobs, Different Rules

Every career comes with specific routines, duties, and workplace guidelines. Read carefully about Maya, Liam, and Carlos to discover how their daily responsibilities differ.

Elena • Supermarket Cashier

Elena works at a busy supermarket in town. She has to arrive early at 7:30 AM to count the money in the cash register. She has to wear a clean store apron and a name badge, and she has to be polite and friendly to every shopper.

However, she doesn't have to carry heavy boxes in the warehouse, and she doesn't have to work late at night on Sundays.

Liam • Graphic Designer

Liam is a freelance digital designer who works completely from home. He has to meet strict client deadlines, and he has to check his client messages every morning.

Luckily, Liam doesn't have to commute in heavy city traffic, and he doesn't have to wear a suit or formal clothes. He can comfortably work in simple jeans and a T-shirt.

Carlos • Restaurant Head Chef

Carlos leads the team in an Italian bistro kitchen. He and his team have to inspect the freshness of fish and vegetables every day. They have to keep their workstations completely clean and sanitized.

Do they have to wash all the dishes themselves? No, they don't! The dishwashing staff takes care of that, so Carlos doesn't have to spend hours at the sink.

Guided Discovery & Rulebook

How to Express Obligations & Rules

Notice how English changes when we speak about obligations (things we must do) versus things that are optional (no obligation).

🔎 Notice the Discovery Clues:

When an action is mandatory / an official rule, we use have to or has to. When an action is not mandatory (you have freedom / choice), what do we say?

✅ HAVE TO / HAS TO = 100% Obligation

It is necessary or a rule. You do not have a choice.
Example: Cashiers have to count the money. Elena has to arrive on time.

❌ DON'T / DOESN'T HAVE TO = 0% Obligation (Optional)

It is NOT necessary. You can do it if you want, but there is no rule requiring it.
Example: Liam doesn't have to wear a necktie at home.

Grammar Table: have to + verb

Notice the base form of the main verb never changes!

Base Verb Form (clean, be, eat, stay...)
Subject Affirmative (+) Negative (−) Question (?) Short Answers
I / You / We / They have to clean the desks. don't have to eat in the lunchroom. Do you have to eat at your desk?
Yes, you do.
No, you don't.
He / She / It has to be very clear. doesn't have to stay outside. Does it have to be so loud in here?
Yes, it does.
No, it doesn't.
Golden Rule: In questions and negatives, the main helper verb is do/does, so we always say have to (NEVER "doesn't has to").
