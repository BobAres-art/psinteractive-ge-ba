Verbs to Describe Trends

In IELTS Task 1, you describe lines that move up, move down, or stay the same. Many verbs have very similar meanings. Here are the 4 main groups:

Group A: Moving Up Normal Rise

Increase • Rise • Climb • Go up

Note: These 4 verbs have almost the same meaning. You can use any of them to describe an upward line!

Graph: Normal Upward Movement
increase → increased
rise → rose (irregular)
climb → climbed
go up → went up
💬 Example: "The number of students increased from 2010 to 2015."
Group B: Very Fast Rise Steep & Sudden

Rocket • Soar

Note: Both mean to go up very high and very fast, like a rocket flying into the sky.

Graph: Very Fast, Steep Jump
rocket → rocketed
soar → soared
💬 Example: "Sales rocketed in July."
Group C: Moving Down Normal Fall

Decrease • Decline • Go down • Drop

Note: These 4 verbs all describe a normal downward movement.

Graph: Normal Downward Movement
decrease → decreased
decline → declined
go down → went down
drop → dropped
💬 Example: "The temperature decreased in the evening."
Group D: Very Fast Fall Big Drop

Plummet • Plunge

Note: Both mean to fall down very fast and by a large amount.

Graph: Sudden Deep Drop
plummet → plummeted
plunge → plunged
💬 Example: "The price of oil plunged in March."

How to Use as a Verb or as a Noun

In English, you can describe the same change in two simple ways: using a verb (action) or using a noun (thing).

Way 1: Using a Verb

Subject + Verb

[The figure for X] + decreased.

Here, decreased is the action verb.

• "The figure for car sales decreased."
• "The number of visitors rose."
• "The price of food dropped."
Way 2: Using a Noun

There was a + Noun + in + [Subject]

There was a decrease in [the figure for X].

Here, a decrease is a noun. Always use in after the noun!

• "There was a decrease in the figure for car sales."
• "There was a rise in the number of visitors."
• "There was a drop in the price of food."

How Fast & How Big: Adjectives and Adverbs

When you want to give more detail, you add Adjectives (with nouns) or Adverbs (with verbs).

Adjective Rule: Before the Noun

There was a significant rise.

"significant" describes the noun "rise".

Adverb Rule: After the Verb

The figure rose significantly.

"significantly" describes the action verb "rose".

See the Differences on Simple Graphs

1. Big Change Large Size

dramatic / significant / substantial / considerable

All these words mean: a very big change.

• Adjective: "a dramatic / significant rise"
• Adverb: "rose dramatically / significantly / substantially"
2. Small Change Little Size

slight / modest

These words mean: a small, small change.

• Adjective: "a slight / modest increase"
• Adverb: "increased slightly / moderately"
3. Speed Speed

rapidly / sharply (Fast) vs. gradually / slowly (Slow)

Describes how fast the line moves.

• Fast: "grew rapidly / sharply"
• Slow: "grew gradually / slowly"
4. Regular Pace Continuous

steady* / consistently

A continuous change with no sudden jumps.

• Adverb: "climbed steadily / consistently"

*Note: "steady" usually goes with continuous progress.

5. Quick Down and Up Special

to dip / a dip

Goes down for a short time, then goes back up.

• Verb: "The numbers dipped in May."
• Noun: "There was a slight dip in May."
6. No Change (Flat) Flat Line

stable / unchanged

The line stays flat with zero increase or decrease.

• "The figure remained stable."
• "Prices remained unchanged."
