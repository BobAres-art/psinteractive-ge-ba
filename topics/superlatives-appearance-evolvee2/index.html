Comparative vs. Superlative

When talking about looks and appearance, remember this simple difference:

Comparative

Comparing 2 People

Formula: adjective + -er than or more + adjective than

• Darius is taller than Liam.

• Leo is more athletic than Sam.

Superlative

Number 1 in a Whole Group (3+)

Formula: the + adjective + -est or the most + adjective

• Darius is the tallest boy in the club.

• Leo is the most athletic runner in our sports team.

Spelling Rules for Appearance Adjectives

Rule 1: 1 Syllable
Add -est

Standard short words add -est.

tall → the tallest

short → the shortest

dark → the darkest

Rule 2: C-V-C
Double the Letter

1 vowel + 1 consonant: double the final letter.

slim → the slimmest

fit → the fittest

thin → the thinnest

Rule 3: Ends in -y
Change -y to -iest

Remove the -y and add -iest.

pretty → the prettiest

curly → the curliest

wavy → the waviest

Rule 4: 2+ Syllables
Use the most

Keep the word the same and place the most before it.

stylish → the most stylish

handsome → the most handsome

Irregular Exceptions (Simple Examples)

These common adjectives don't use -est. Learn these two simple patterns:

good → better → the best Irregular

Simple appearance example:

"Noah has the best haircut in our class."
bad → worse → the worst Irregular

Simple appearance example:

"Jack had the worst posture before he started gym training."
